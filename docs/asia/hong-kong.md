## 3. Language and register

### 3a. Cantonese, standard written Chinese (書面語) or English

Facts:
- In the 2021 Census, 88.2% of residents aged 5 and over gave Cantonese as their usual spoken language. Putonghua was 2.3% and English 4.6% (secondary summary) [S20].
- Written Cantonese appears in adverts and billboards, tabloids, chat and social networking sites, and some subtitles. News and most formal writing use standard written Chinese [S13].
- An HK agency guide (2026): formal content goes in standard written Chinese with Traditional characters. Brands should use written Cantonese "sparingly". Mixing English into Cantonese "can feel authentic" on social media. Its example: 「今日 lunch 去咗間新開嘅 cafe，好正！」 Simplified characters read as mainland and can alienate local readers [S15].

House-style patterns (UNVERIFIED: observation, not a published source):

| Level | What it looks like | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| A. 書面語 | No Cantonese characters: 的/了/在/不/沒有/他們 | Statements, apologies, T&Cs, legal, lucky-draw rules, regulated claims |
| B. 港式中文 | Written-Chinese base with HK words (質素, 上載, 著數, 減價), a little English (book位, confirm) and an occasional 嘅/咗 | Brand captions, carousel text, headlines, e-commerce posts |
| C. 粵語 | Fully colloquial: 嘅 咗 喺 唔 冇 啲 嘢 佢哋 | Reel voiceover, Stories text, replies, DMs, WhatsApp, Threads |

- Pick one level per piece and hold it. Readers notice mixing written and spoken registers mid-sentence, and some read it as a fake-account or scam sign (UNVERIFIED).
- The usual pattern for a brand caption is a level-B body with one level-C punchline line (UNVERIFIED).
- English goes into Cantonese grammar without changing form. It is lowercase with no spaces: 「book咗位」「send個file俾你」「好多politics」「support唔到你」. Keep everyday words (OK, book, confirm, WhatsApp as a verb, like, share, follow, OT, deadline, discount, free). Translate abstract PowerPoint English (mindset, framework, journey, leverage, empower) back into Cantonese (UNVERIFIED).
- Layout: captions often have no full stops, one idea per line, ‼️ used as punctuation, emoji at the end of a clause, and a 【】 headline line on brand and e-commerce posts: 【每朝9點開搶⚡】 (UNVERIFIED).

### 3b. Reel voiceovers: Cantonese is the default

Spoken Reels are Cantonese unless the brand is English-first (INFERENCE from [S20]). A script reads as "translated from Mandarin" when only the characters were swapped (的→嘅, 是→係) and the word order stayed Mandarin.

| Natural Cantonese | Translated-from-Mandarin tell | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 你行先 / 食咗飯先 / 等我諗吓先 (先 after the verb) | 你先走 / 先吃飯 | Postverbal 先 is a hallmark of Cantonese; Mandarin has no equivalent position [S16] |
| 畀本書我 (thing before person) | 畀我本書 / 給我一本書 | [S17] |
| 佢高過我 (adjective + 過) | 佢比我高 | [S17] |
| 嘅 咗 喺 唔 冇 啲 嘢 佢 哋 | 的 了 在 不 沒有 一點 東西 他 們 | Cantonese-to-standard mapping [S13] |

Particles. Each one needs a job; stacking them is a classic AI failure (UNVERIFIED observation).

| Particle | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 喎 wo3 / wo5 | Realising something, reminding, passing on hearsay (wo5 = heard it, may not agree) | 原來今日有得食喎 [S18] |
| 囉 lo1 | The obvious conclusion, often with 咪 | 冇車咪搭的士囉 [S18] |
| 㗎 gaa3 | 嘅+呀, stresses a fact | 呢個真係好食㗎 [S17][S18] |
| 啫 / 咋 | Only, downplaying | 十蚊啫 / 試吓咋 [S18] |
| 啦 laa1 / laa3 | laa1 softens a request; laa3 marks a change of state | 快啲book啦 / 開咗售啦 [S18] |

Common AI mistakes (UNVERIFIED: observed in AI drafts):
- Taiwanese particles (喔 唷 欸 耶 齁)
- Mainland buzzwords (賦能 內卷 種草 打工人 爆款 絕絕子)
- Written-Chinese verbs inside speech (進行 能夠 及 決策者)
- Calques such as 「我入面等你」
- A particle after every clause
- Taiwanese vocabulary (線上 品質 貼文 專案), because most Traditional-Chinese training text is Taiwanese

## 4. Hook and caption clichés

What the evidence says:
- Instagram does not recommend content "users broadly tell us they dislike, including clickbait, engagement bait, or which promotes a contest or giveaway". It also says to post original content, remove third-party watermarks, and keep Reels at 3 minutes or less to reach people who don't follow you [S21]. The typical HK giveaway post (「follow + tag 3個朋友 + share落story 就有機會贏」) is therefore engagement bait. It will not reach non-followers, and if winners are drawn by chance it needs a TPCL (§6).
- Several HK price clichés carry legal risk under the Trade Descriptions Ordinance guidelines [S23]:
  - 「全港最平」: needs evidence (para 2.18).
  - 「原價$X 而家$Y」: the original price must have actually been charged for a reasonable period (2.16).
  - 「全場半價」: must be true for every item (2.19).
  - 「低至X折」: if fewer than about 10% of items get the top cut, say they are selected items (2.19).
  - Hidden add-on charges are a risk (2.20).
- Health hooks such as 「排毒」「調節荷爾蒙」「穩定血糖」 trigger the Cap 231 rules (§6) [S26].
- Fake 「朋友仔推介」 posts, or staff and agents posting as customers, can be a misleading-omission offence (para 3.42) [S23].

Low-effort hooks on HK IG (INFERENCE; no published study found):
- 「你一定唔知…」「原來…」「竟然…」
- 「全港首間」「隱世」「必食／必去／必試」
- 「中伏？」 used as bait
- 「超正🔥🔥🔥」 emoji walls
- 「仲未試？」 and 「邊個話…」 openers with no payoff
- Copy that is 100% emoji and adjectives with no price, place or number

What works instead (INFERENCE unless cited):
- Specifics people can check: price, district, MTR exit, queue time, portion size
- A user-post feel, since user content is the most trusted kind [S5]
- Original footage [S21]
- Self-deprecating local humour; level B or C register held consistently (UNVERIFIED)
- On Threads: text-first gossip and workplace angles [S8]

## 5. Comment, reply and DM culture

- **Tone.** Reply in the commenter's register: Cantonese to Cantonese, English to English. Keep replies short and in level C. Brand replies drop most particles (UNVERIFIED).
- **「價錢PM」「PM我」「inbox」.** These are common (words.hk lists inbox as a verb for private messages, as opposed to public comments [S12]).
  - No HK rule found that requires a price in the post (UNVERIFIED).
  - Once a DM quotes a price it is an "invitation to purchase" under the TDO. The trader's identity, business address, price including taxes, and delivery charges must then be available (s.13E(4), guidelines 3.18–3.21) [S23] (INFERENCE that this applies to DMs).
  - Hidden prices plus 「轉WhatsApp傾」 is also the scammer pattern: scammers move the chat off-platform and push for bank transfers [S10].
  - Default (INFERENCE): show a price or price range publicly, put one official WhatsApp number in the bio, and never ask buyers to move to an unlisted number.
- **「入blog睇」.** This is a Facebook- and blog-era phrase. On IG, write 「link喺bio」「詳情睇bio」 (INFERENCE, UNVERIFIED).
- **WhatsApp hand-off.** This is the expected service channel [S9]. Saving a commenter's or buyer's phone number to send them promotions is direct marketing under the PDPO, and needs notice and consent first (§6) [S32].
- **Emoji.** Used at clause ends as a tone marker (‼️ urgent, 🔥 a good deal, 🥹 pleading, 🙏🏻), often with skin-tone modifiers. HK-style 「😂😂😂」 runs are normal in replies and wrong in statements (UNVERIFIED).
- **Public complaints.** Acknowledge the complaint once, publicly, then move the details to DM or WhatsApp. Formal statements and apologies go in level-A 書面語 (UNVERIFIED house style, consistent with [S13] on formal writing). Do not argue in Cantonese slang under a complaint (INFERENCE).

## 8. Currency and number conventions

- The currency is the Hong Kong dollar: HK$ (code HKD). The formal Chinese name is 港元, as used by the HKMA. It is pegged within HK$7.75–7.85 per US$1 [S46].
- In local texts a bare 「$」 means HK$: government writing has 「$5,000,000」 [S35] and 「HK$1,590」 [S34]. On IG, write $99 or HK$99. Never write ¥ or RMB unless you mean RMB, and never write NT$.
- Colloquial: 蚊 is only used after a number (「一百蚊」「十蚊啫」) [S12]. 元 is formal and 港幣 is common in speech (UNVERIFIED).
- Discounts: 「八折」 means paying 80%, which is 20% off. 「六折」 means paying 60% [S12]. Do not write "20% off" as 「二折」; that means paying 20%. Also common: 「半價」, 「買一送一」 [S15], 「第二件半價」 (UNVERIFIED).
- Big numbers: Chinese copy counts in 萬 (10,000) and 億 (100,000,000), e.g. 「$1.2萬」 or 「$12,000」. Casual social copy also uses English 「k」, e.g. 「10k fol」 (UNVERIFIED). Never convert to a US "million" scale without the 萬 equivalent (INFERENCE).
- Dates: GovHK English writes the day first (「1 January」) and Chinese as 「1月1日 星期四」 [S38]. HKO writes 「2026年1月1日」 [S39]. For numeric dates use DD/MM, never US MM/DD (UNVERIFIED as a written standard; INFERENCE from the day-first English usage).
- Mixing: Arabic numerals are the norm in captions, with no space between Chinese text and numbers or English: 「$99兩碗」「8萬件」 (UNVERIFIED).
